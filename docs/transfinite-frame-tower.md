# Scott sentences at successor cardinals and countable limits

The first application is a countable structure $M$ in which every singleton has disjointifying rank at most $2$. Assuming $\square_{\aleph_1}$, its Scott sentence has a model of size $\aleph_2$, all its models have size at most $\aleph_2$, and some singleton of $M$ has rank exactly $2$. The countable structure and its rank bound are independent of square. Square supplies the large model. [Rank-two theorem](../KnightModel/RankTwo/ScottSpectrum.lean)

The iteration distinguishes two questions. For every countable ordinal $\beta$ one can construct a countable structure whose singleton ranks are at most $\beta$ and whose Scott sentence characterizes $\aleph_\beta$, assuming $\square_{\aleph_\gamma}$ for $0<\gamma<\beta$. The initial jump uses $\square_{\aleph_0}$, which holds in ZFC. Here *characterizes* means that this cardinal is attained and no larger model exists. If one instead fixes a singleton rank bound $\alpha$ and asks for the **largest possible cardinal**, the sharp answer under the corresponding square hypotheses is

$$
U_\alpha =
\begin{cases}
\aleph_\alpha & \alpha<\omega,\\
\aleph_{\alpha+1} & \omega\leq\alpha<\omega_1.
\end{cases}
$$

The upper bounds hold without square. For sharpness at an infinite $\alpha$, the large model uses $\square_{\aleph_\gamma}$ for $0<\gamma\leq\alpha$, including the jump at $\aleph_\alpha$. The first difference occurs at $\omega$. The limit structure has a model of size $\aleph_\omega$ and every one of its finite tuples has finite rank. Its successor can have a model of size $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ with singleton ranks at most $\omega$. Each comparison at the successor only has to give *some* finite residual rank, which may depend on the pair being compared. The public Lean statements are `exists_scott_characterizing_aleph` and `exists_sharp_scott_characterization`. The first limit case is stated separately as `exists_rank_omega_scott_characterization`. ## What a successor must preserve

The input is a countable relational structure $A$ with a named linear order on its **whole carrier** and a named point $i$. A large realization is a model $B$ of its Scott sentence of size $\kappa$, together with an increasing sequence $(b_\xi)_{\xi<\kappa}$ in the named order. The countable starting structure is a well-order of type $\omega$ with every point individually named. Its automorphism group fixes every tuple. [Seed and realization](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Seed.lean) · [Starting structure](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Zero.lean)

Write $I$ for the old ordered carrier on either side. Form the rational vector group $V=\bigoplus_{x\in I}\mathbb Q$, ordered by the coefficient at the greatest index where two vectors differ. The unit vector at the marked point supplies a distinguished positive vector. The word order $W$ consists of finite lists of nonnegative vectors. Letters at the first difference decide the order, and a proper extension comes before its prefix, so the empty word is greatest. A *frame* is an order embedding of $W$ onto a principal initial segment. In the countable structure take the proper frames described by finite word schemes with vector parameters. The three sorts are old elements, vectors, and frames. Relations retain the old language, name vector coefficients, and record the equation $u=v\circ c$ for a transition program $c$. [Words](../KnightModel/RankTwo/Words.lean) · [Schemes](../KnightModel/RankTwo/WordSchemes.lean) · [Successor structure](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SuccessorModel.lean)

## How to get the model of size $\kappa^+$

The obstacle is to make $\kappa^+$ increasing frame endpoints while keeping changes between **every pair** of their charts describable by the same finite schemes used on the countable side. The following construction starts from $B$ and clubs $C_\delta\subseteq\delta$ at the nonzero limit ordinals below $\kappa^+$. Each club has order type at most $\kappa$, and $C_\gamma=C_\delta\cap\gamma$ at its proper accumulation points. These are the features of Jensen's $\square_\kappa$ used by the proof. At successor ordinals use the singleton predecessor. [Equivalence with Jensen square](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Square.lean) · [Square at $\aleph_0$](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SquareOmega.lean) · [Limit-club formulation](../KnightModel/RankTwo/WalkSquareLimit.lean)

1. **Make one row of words for each ordinal.** From $b>a$, step to the least member of $C_b$ greater than or equal to $a$. Record its position $\xi$ in $C_b$ as the letter given by the unit vector at $b_\xi$ in the old model. Continue to $a$. Ordinals decrease at each step, so the record $w_b(a)$ is finite. For each $b$, the row $a\mapsto w_b(a)$ is strictly increasing on $a\leq b$, and $w_b(b)$ is empty. This also explains the singular case. Even when a club has order type exactly $\kappa$, the position of **each of its members** is strictly below $\kappa$. The old sequence supplies every letter the walk requests. [Walk rows](../KnightModel/RankTwo/WalkRows.lean) · [Slot labels](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Atlas.lean)

2. **Glue the rows into one ordered set.** The sector $S_{b,a}$ consists of words at most $w_b(a)$ and strictly above every earlier mark in row $b$. Form the ordered sum of the native sectors $S_{a,a}$, one for each $a<\kappa^+$. Let $e_b$ be the final point of its $b$-th sector. To chart the segment through $e_b$, transport each $S_{a,a}$ with $a\le b$ onto $S_{b,a}$. The latter sectors partition $W$, so these maps glue to an isomorphism from the segment through $e_b$ onto $W$. Square coherence makes the transport describable. On a final interval below a limit, changing rows adds a fixed prefix to the recorded walk. The possible fold at the endpoint and the transports on earlier intervals give finite changes of coordinates. [Sectors and charts](../KnightModel/RankTwo/Atlas.lean) · [Finite prefix rules](../KnightModel/RankTwo/WalkPieces.lean) · [Sector transports](../KnightModel/RankTwo/AtlasWalk.lean)

3. **Count actual frames.** The inverse charts are frames with strictly increasing endpoints $e_b$. Close these frames under the coordinate programs. There are $\kappa^+$ anchors and at most $\kappa$ programs over the old model, since there are countably many finite schemes and $|V|\leq\kappa$. This gives exactly $\kappa^+$ frames and the same cardinality for the whole relational structure. In particular $\kappa=\aleph_1$ gives the $\aleph_2$ model. [Frame cardinality](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Atlas.lean) · [Whole-model cardinality](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SuccessorModel.lean)

4. **Check the Scott sentence.** Start from the back-and-forth on the two old structures. Retain a pair of anchor frames and describe each selected frame below its anchor by a program. A vector move adds its finitely many support indices to the old match and copies its rational coefficients. Word and program moves then agree on both sides. For a move above the current anchor, solve the prescribed transition from a new anchor down to the retained one by pasting the maps on the initial segment and its complementary tail. All previously matched frames keep their coordinates after this change of anchor. The resulting back-and-forth works in both directions and preserves all the named relations. [Program evaluation](../KnightModel/Transfinite/TransferRun.lean) · [Anchor moves](../KnightModel/RankTwo/ScottFrames.lean) · [Successor back-and-forth](../KnightModel/Transfinite/TransferFrames.lean)

## Why the rank grows slowly

Use the comparison clause of disjointifying rank on **finite tuples**. For a group $H$ acting on $t$, rank zero means that $H$ fixes $t$. At positive $\alpha$ compare $t$ with every conjugate $t'$. Either $t$ over $H_{t'}$ or $t'$ over $H_t$ must have rank below $\alpha$. Restricting the group preserves an upper bound. Two objects with the same stabilizer inside $H$ have the same comparison bounds. On a singleton this supplies an upper bound for the original disjointifying rank, which also permits a witness clause. [Comparison rank](../KnightModel/Transfinite/Comparison.lean)

Take two finite tuples. Choose a frame $v$ whose endpoint is greatest among their frame entries, and fix the tuple containing $v$. Every frame $u$ in the other tuple has the form $v\circ c$. Choose a named scheme representing $c$. Its admissible parameter tuples form a nonempty semilinear set. Semilinear selection gives a parameter tuple fixed by every automorphism fixing $u,v$. Conversely, $v$ and the parameters determine $u$. The ordered finite support of a vector is recoverable pointwise, since the only increasing permutation of a finite linear order is the identity. Combining these supports with the old entries gives one finite old tuple with exactly the stabilizer of the tuple being compared, relative to the fixed tuple. If both tuples contain only old elements and vectors, the same support argument applies directly. Thus **old tuple ranks strictly below $\alpha$ give new tuple ranks at most $\alpha$**. This works for limit and successor $\alpha$. [Canonical selection](../KnightModel/RankTwo/SemilinearRank.lean) · [Finite supports](../KnightModel/Transfinite/CanonicalFrame.lean) · [Successor rank](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SuccessorRank.lean)

For another jump we need an invariant order on the new carrier. Compare the values of two frames first at the empty word, then at successive word lengths. At a fixed length the two strict signs are semilinear. In one variable the first sign is positive if some input has positive sign and no earlier input has negative sign. Finite unions of intervals and points make this comparison total for unequal functions. Iterating the one-variable test handles several letters. The empty word tests the endpoints, so the order contains the increasing sequence of $\kappa^+$ anchors. Its invariance lets a simultaneous source lift of a target pair determine the target order independently of the lift. This gives the next seed its named whole-carrier order, with the old points first, then the vectors, then the frames. [Frame order](../KnightModel/Transfinite/FrameOrder.lean) · [Automorphism invariance](../KnightModel/Transfinite/FrameOrderInvariant.lean) · [Ordered successor realization](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SuccessorScott.lean)

The same support calculation gives a direct spectrum bound for a single jump over any seed. In an arbitrary model of the new Scott sentence the named old sort satisfies the old Scott sentence. Suppose it has size at most $\kappa$. For each point $y$, collect the points determined by $y$ and some finite tuple of old elements. There are at most $\kappa$ such tuples and countably many rank-zero labels per tuple, each with at most one realization. The preceding comparison covers each pair in one direction by these sets. The counting argument below gives a bound of $\kappa^+$ on the entire model. [Successor spectrum bound](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SuccessorBound.lean)

## Limits and the cardinal ceiling

At a nonzero countable limit $\lambda$, enumerate its earlier stages by the natural numbers. This supplies a countable family cofinal in $\lambda$. Take their disjoint sum with each component named, ordered lexicographically by component and then by its own order. The large realizations have a componentwise back-and-forth with the countable sum. Their sizes have supremum $\aleph_\lambda$, and their ordered sequences provide an increasing sequence of that length. [Limit seed](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SumSeed.lean) · [Increasing sequence](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SumOrder.lean)

A finite tuple may meet several components. For coordinates of comparison ranks bounded by $a,b$, the proof uses the ordinal measure $2\cdot\max(a,b)+\mathbf{1}_{a=b}$, with multiplication on the left. Compare a coordinate with maximal bound and fix the whole opposite pair in the direction that lowers that coordinate. The other bound does not increase. If the two maxima were tied, the tie disappears. Otherwise the maximum drops. In either case the measure decreases. For $a,b<\lambda$ this measure is below $\lambda$, because $2\cdot\lambda=\lambda$ for every nonzero limit. Iterating the pair result gives a strict bound below $\lambda$ for each finite tuple, even when no common smaller bound works for all tuples. [Pair and tuple estimates](../KnightModel/Transfinite/ComparisonLimit.lean) · [Application to named sums](../KnightModel/Transfinite/SumRank.lean)

Finally, a *label* records the orbit of a finite tuple in the countable source. Over a fixed finite tuple in a Scott model, each rank-zero label has at most one realization. At a positive bound $\alpha$, let $K=\sup_{\eta<\alpha}U_\eta$. The lower-rank labels over a given tuple have at most $K$ realizations altogether, since there are only countably many labels. The witness clause bounds a whole label fiber this way. In the comparison clause, each point $y$ of the fiber has a set $L_y$ of size at most $K$, and each pair $x,y$ satisfies $x\in L_y$ or $y\in L_x$. If the fiber exceeded $K^+$, choose $K^+$ of its points, then another point outside the union of their sets. Its own set would have to contain all the chosen points. Thus $U_0=\aleph_0$ and $U_\alpha=K^+$ at positive ranks give universal upper bounds. The construction attains them under square. If all singleton ranks were strictly below $\alpha>0$, counting their labels would give size at most $K$. A realization of size $K^+$ therefore forces a singleton of rank exactly $\alpha$.
